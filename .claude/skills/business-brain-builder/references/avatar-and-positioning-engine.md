# The avatar and positioning engine

## What this is and why it exists

Krista's own Dream Client Blueprint prompt takes **three inputs** and generates an entire Ideal Customer Profile plus a full authority positioning set. Three questions in, roughly twenty pages of usable work out.

Files 03 and 04 used to ask fourteen questions between them. That was the single biggest time sink in the whole interview and it produced worse answers, because a person describing their own client one narrow question at a time gives you fragments, while a model working from their market, their offer and their transformation gives you a coherent person.

Krista's instruction, 2026-08-25: *"ask them a few questions, just like this prompt does, and then you have the output, so you can give them everything back so they can see it."*

**Three questions. Then generate. Then show it back. That is the whole method.**

---

## THE THREE QUESTIONS

Ask these one at a time. Nothing else. Do not add follow-ups, do not probe, do not ask a clarifying question unless an answer is genuinely unusable.

**1. Who do you serve?**

> "Who do you serve? Be as specific as you can, and include what kind of shape they are usually in when they come to you."

**2. What do you do for them?**

> "And what do you actually do for them? Not your job title. The thing they get."

**3. The one-liner.**

> "Last one. Finish this sentence: I specialize in helping ______ solve ______."

**Then two optional lifts, offered together, never pushed:**

> "Two optional things that make this sharper if you have them. Anything you would call your unique mechanism, meaning the thing you do that nobody else does. And a website or landing page I can read. Skip either if you do not have them."

If they have a URL, read it. It usually answers more than the three questions did.

That is the intake. Move to generation.

---

## WHAT YOU GENERATE FROM THOSE THREE ANSWERS

Generate in this order, because the positioning is built out of the avatar and reads badly if you do it the other way round. The file numbers stay as they are; only the build order changes.

### Part one, into `04-ideal-client.md` (standalone) or `02-Ideal-Client.md` (kit mode)

Build the full profile across all seven areas:

1. **Who they are.** Work, age, where they live, family situation, one or two lines describing them, what their lifestyle actually feels like.
2. **What they are struggling with.** Main frustration, what they already tried that failed, their five biggest fears, how those fears touch their relationships and confidence and money, what they say in their head that nobody hears, what they absolutely refuse to do to fix it, and three to five soundbites of that refusal in their own words.
3. **What they have already tried.** Five or six specific things, each with a casual quote of how they would describe the failure.
4. **What they actually want.** If it were fixed overnight, five outcomes that would change their world, what success would feel like, what they would be proud to say out loud.
5. **The emotions.** Five they feel now, five they want to feel, and what they are really chasing underneath.
6. **What is blocking them.** Five objections to hiring this person, what the market tells them they need, what they believe they must sacrifice, who they blame, their limiting beliefs, and what they secretly get out of staying stuck.
7. **Why this person.** Their method, why it is smarter than what the client tried, what clients say afterwards, what the results prove, what they offer that nobody else does.

### Part two, into `03-unique-differentiator.md` (standalone) or `01-Brand-Brain.md` (kit mode)

Now use the profile you just built:

1. **Differentiation audit.** The typical competitors in their market. The top five generic claims everyone in that space makes. Where their own description from question two sounds like everyone else, rewritten specific.
2. **Three value propositions.** Version A clear and direct with no hype. Version B premium and authoritative. Version C simple and conversational. Each names who they help, the painful problem, the mechanism, and the transformation.
3. **The unique mechanism, named.** What it is, why it works, and what it replaces, framed as old way versus new way. If they did not give you one, infer it from their answers and label it `PROPOSED, CONFIRM THIS`.
4. **The positioning set.** A one-sentence authority statement, a two to three sentence "what I do" paragraph, a website hero with headline and subhead and three bullets, a short bio of 75 to 120 words, and a long bio of 200 to 300 words.

### Part three, held for later files, not shown now

Generate these at the same time and hold them. They are the reason files 10 and 11 later go fast.

- **Five to seven messaging pillars.** For each: what this person believes, what the audience currently believes instead, three content angles, five example hooks, three CTAs that are not salesy. → feeds `10-best-content.md`
- **Ten objections in the client's exact words**, each with a reframe and one proof idea. → feeds `10-best-content.md` and the AI context file

Say nothing about these when you show the work back. They surface when those files come up, already done.

## HOW YOU SHOW IT BACK

This is the moment the whole thing is built around. They answered three questions and are about to see their business described better than they have ever described it themselves.

Show the avatar first, in full. Then say:

> "That is who you are talking to. Everything from here writes to that person.
>
> Now what makes you different."

Show the positioning set. Then **one question, not a list**:

> "Anything in there wrong?"

Offer the answers as things they tap:

```
nothing, that's right
```

```
one thing is off
```

If they say something is off, fix that one thing and move on. **Do not walk them through it section by section asking for approval.** Do not ask if they want to adjust the bio, then the hero, then the pillars. One pass, one question, fix what they name, move.

---

## RULES

- **Three questions. Not four, not eight.** If you are tempted to ask a clarifying question, generate instead and let them correct it. A wrong guess they fix in five seconds beats a right question that costs two minutes.
- **Never invent a credential, a number, a client result or a testimonial.** Everything in the avatar is a reasoned inference about a person who does not exist yet, which is fine. Anything about THEIR business that would be a factual claim is not. Where you need one and do not have it, write `ADD REAL LANGUAGE HERE LATER`.
- **Label inference as inference** where it matters, using `PROPOSED, CONFIRM THIS`, and nowhere else. Do not litter the file with hedges.
- **If they came in with an avatar document or a differentiator document already, that document wins.** Structure it, do not regenerate it, do not re-interview. More than one avatar is normal; keep each as its own labeled section.
- **Quality bar: if the profile could describe anyone, it is wrong.** It needs a real income range, real financial tension, a life stage, timing pressure, actual internal dialogue written as quotes, and at least one emotional contradiction. Regenerate before showing it rather than showing something generic.
- **In kit mode this all still lands in the Brand-System files** per `kit-brand-system-mapping.md`, and the inherited sections stay untouched.
